# Crypto order flow, intraday/weekly seasonality, short-horizon reversal/momentum, and cross-sectional factors (2018-2026)

Method note: these notes come from about 25 web searches made on 2026-10-15. Direct page fetches (WebFetch/curl to arXiv, SSRN, Quantpedia, Springer and others) were blocked by the sandbox network policy. Every figure below therefore comes from search-result abstracts and excerpts, not full-text tables. Exact thresholds and cost assumptions that the snippets did not show are listed under Gaps rather than guessed. Anything marked "unverified" came only from a secondary summary.

Project data: Binance USD-M perps, 20 large coins, 2020-2026, with 1-minute OHLCV, trade counts and taker-buy volume, plus the 5-minute taker buy/sell ratio. Those fields are enough to build per-bar taker imbalance, i.e. (2*takerBuyVol - Vol)/Vol. They are not enough for order-book depth, true VPIN with volume buckets from tick data (only an approximation from 1-minute bars is possible), or funding-rate data unless the project already holds funding separately.

## Q1. Which order-flow imbalance signals (signed volume, taker buy ratio, VPIN-like toxicity) predict crypto returns at minutes-to-days horizons, net of costs?

### Takeaway
At minutes to an hour, the documented taker-flow effects are real but too small to trade. Reversal after taker-driven bars earns about 1.3 bp gross against about 5 bp round-trip cost. The most promising lead is opening order imbalance at quarter-hour marks on Binance perps predicting 4-12 hour returns. It is only an in-sample association so far, and an independent practitioner rebuild titled its write-up "Real Pattern, No Trade". VPIN predicts BTC jumps (volatility), not direction.

### Cited Findings
**Strategy OF-1: Quarter-hour opening order imbalance (Binance perps)**
- "The Quarter-Hour Effect: Periodic Algorithmic Trading and Return Predictability in Cryptocurrency Futures" (Kim and Hansen, arXiv 2607.09426, July 2026). Data: six Binance USDT-margined perpetuals, 1 Jan 2021 to 31 Oct 2024. Volume and activity bursts cluster at quarter-hour marks (:00/:15/:30/:45), which the authors attribute to periodic algorithmic trading — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09426); [Microstructure Lab summary](https://themicrostructurelab.substack.com/p/the-quarter-hour-clock-in-crypto)
- Opening order imbalance at those marks forecasts returns over the next 4 to 12 hours, with much weaker effects at finer clock-time frequencies. The authors warn the pattern should not be read as causal price impact — [arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.09426); [arXiv pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.09426)
- The out-of-sample claim covers only the very short "opening return" (opening 10-second return) forecasts. The best model combines lagged quarter-hour opening returns with 28 technical indicators and reaches an average OOS R² of 3.37% and AUC of 0.6011 across six contracts. The OOS evaluation starts 1 July 2021. The order-imbalance regressions are in-sample predictive associations only — [Pith summary](https://pith.science/paper/2607.09426); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.09426v2)
- The paper acknowledges an independent data validation and replication study by Wade Kimbrough — [arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.09426)
- Practitioner critique: The Microstructure Lab rebuilt the pattern from every Binance trade since 2023, scored six of the paper's claims and tested the 12-hour signal against real trades. The post is titled "The Quarter-Hour Clock in Crypto Futures: Real Pattern, No Trade". The snippets did not show its numbers — [Substack](https://themicrostructurelab.substack.com/p/the-quarter-hour-clock-in-crypto)
- Testable rule with project data: use 1-minute taker-buy volume. Compute imbalance = (takerBuy - takerSell)/(takerBuy + takerSell) in the first minute after each :00/:15/:30/:45. Use it as a sign signal for a 4-12 hour hold. One open-source bot has an issue proposing to derive this from Binance aggTrades for majors from 2024-11 onward, which suggests practitioners are trying it — [GitHub issue](https://github.com/s10023/buibui-moon-trader-bot/issues/937)

**Strategy OF-2: Short-horizon reversal after taker-flow-driven bars**
- "Short-horizon mean reversion in cryptocurrency markets: a matched cross-market measurement" (arXiv 2608.21888, 2026). At 15-minute horizons, 90% of 183 Binance pairs show significant directional reversal, against 2.7% of 187 US stocks/ETFs. The result survives an artifact battery, an exact permutation null and a frozen six-month holdout — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21888); [Pith](https://pith.science/paper/2608.21888)
- Mechanism: reversal follows moves driven by aggressive taker flow and grows with flow intensity. It does not depend on order-book depth consumed, which the authors read as compensation to liquidity providers. Taker imbalance comes from Binance kline taker-buy volume, which is the same field the project holds — [arXiv pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.21888)
- Effect size is tiny. OOS AUC is 0.540 after flow-driven bars vs 0.519 after flow-opposed bars. The OOS AUC gap is +0.031 as designed and +0.011 (95% CI +0.008 to +0.014) under the most conservative accounting. In walk-forward tests, taker-imbalance lags add nothing beyond lagged signs (OOS AUC 0.529 in both cases) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.21888); [Pith](https://pith.science/paper/2608.21888)
- Net of costs: the gross edge is about 1.3 bp per trade against a cheapest round trip of about 5 bp. The authors conclude it is "large enough to detect, too small to clear" spot costs — [Pith](https://pith.science/paper/2608.21888)
- A practitioner blog reports a crypto backtest Sharpe of 13.4 that disappeared once trading costs were added. This is anecdotal but consistent with the paper — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/pavel_kkkkazantsev/my-crypto-backtest-had-a-sharpe-of-134-then-i-added-trading-costs-5718)

**Strategy OF-3: VPIN / order-flow toxicity**
- "Bitcoin wild moves: Evidence from order flow toxicity and price jumps" (Kitvanitphasu et al., Research in International Business and Finance vol. 81, 2026; online 2025). In a VAR on high-frequency data, VPIN significantly predicts future BTC price jumps, and the effect accumulates over time. Feedback from jumps to VPIN is weaker. The interpretation is behavioural (herding, momentum trading) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0275531925004192); [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/riibaf/v81y2026ics0275531925004192.html)
- Related BTC evidence: informed trading lowers BTC volatility, but its effect on returns depends on whether buy-side or sell-side informed trading dominates — [MDPI JRFM 2026](https://www.mdpi.com/1911-8074/19/1/59)
- Use: VPIN works as a volatility/jump-risk filter (for example, cutting exposure or widening stops when VPIN is high), not as a directional alpha. No source found tests VPIN as a directional return predictor for crypto.

**Other order-flow and volume evidence**
- "Disagreement and returns: The case of cryptocurrencies" (Garfinkel et al., Financial Management, 2025). Abnormal trading volume, a disagreement proxy, is negatively related to expected cross-sectional crypto returns on Binance data. Volume- and trade-based order-imbalance measures enter mainly as controls — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/fima.12491)
- "Fundamentals of Perpetual Futures" (arXiv 2212.06888) uses hourly Binance signed taker imbalance, defined as (takerBuy$ - takerSell$)/(sum). It shows the flow leans against perp-index deviations, which is not return prediction — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.06888)
- Tick-level ML on five coins finds the effect of order-flow imbalance on returns is mostly monotone, with concavity at extremes — [arXiv 2602.00776](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.00776v1)
- An informal Medium study of perp "meta-order flow" reports buy-pressure continuation (momentum) at short horizons. This is the opposite sign to OF-2, the study is not peer reviewed, and it should be treated as anecdotal — [Medium/Coinmonks](https://medium.com/coinmonks/meta-order-flow-in-crypto-perps-catching-big-whale-6a127e2f70e8)

### Inferences
- The project's 1-minute taker-buy volume is exactly the input used by OF-1 and OF-2, so both can be replicated directly. OF-2 is pre-shown to be uneconomic at taker costs. It would only become viable as a maker or limit-order strategy, which the backtest engine probably cannot model credibly.
- OF-1 at 4-12 hours is the only order-flow signal with a horizon long enough for perp taker fees (about 4-5 bp per side) to matter little. It has no OOS evidence for the imbalance component, and a public practitioner rebuild found no tradable edge. Flag it as high data-mining risk, but cheap to test: pre-register a single rule (sign of :00 opening-minute imbalance, hold 8h) and evaluate it on 2025-2026 data after the paper's sample ends in Oct 2024.
- At daily horizon, the evidence points to abnormal volume as a negative cross-sectional predictor (Garfinkel). Taker ratio itself has no published daily-horizon cross-sectional test that I found.

### Gaps
- Microstructure Lab's actual trade results (Sharpe or PnL of the 12-hour signal) were not visible in the snippets.
- The Quarter-Hour paper's exact imbalance definition, coefficient sizes and any Sharpe figures were not visible.
- No peer-reviewed study found tests the Binance 5-minute "taker buy/sell ratio" endpoint, or daily taker ratio, as a return predictor net of costs.
- No paper found tests VPIN as a directional signal in crypto.

## Q2. What intraday/weekly seasonal patterns (US open, 21-23 UTC, Asian session, funding times 00/08/16 UTC, weekend, Monday, turn-of-month) are documented, and do they persist after 2021?

### Takeaway
Activity seasonality (volume, volatility, spreads peaking 15-17 UTC; low weekend volume) is robust across exchanges and coins. Return seasonality is fragile. The 21-23 UTC BTC window comes from Gemini data ending in February 2022, and it had a rough 2022-2023. Monday and day-of-week effects fail after 2015 in the most careful recent test (Mueller 2024), and turn-of-month evidence is inconsistent. The newer and better-documented leads are: (a) the BTC overnight-vs-NYSE-hours split, where returns accrue outside US cash hours; (b) Concretum's "Monday Asia Open" window for intraday trend strength (gross Sharpe only); (c) quarter-hour microstructure.

### Cited Findings
**21:00-23:00 UTC BTC window (already a project candidate). Who documents it, and is it explained?**
- Padysak & Vojtko (2022), "Seasonality, Trend-following, and Mean reversion in Bitcoin" (SSRN 4081000). Hourly Gemini BTC data from Oct 2015 to early 2022. The strongest hourly returns fall at 22:00 and 23:00 UTC, so the rule is to hold BTC only 21:00-23:00 UTC. The paper also finds that BTC trends near its highs and rebounds near its lows — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4081000); [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/the-seasonality-of-bitcoin/)
- Vojtko & Javorská (2023), "The Seasonality of Bitcoin", Int. J. of Cryptocurrency Research 3(2):26-35. Same Gemini sample (9 Oct 2015 to 3 Feb 2022, UTC). Rule: buy 21:00 UTC, sell 23:00 UTC — [Svedberg Open PDF](https://www.svedbergopen.com/files/1706875198_(2)_IJCCR202314071414SK_(p_26-35).pdf); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/378110832_The_Seasonality_of_Bitcoin)
- Reported performance: a third-party summary cites a Sharpe of about 1.58 for the two-hour strategy (unverified). An EdgeTools TradingView implementation reports about 33% annual return, about 21% volatility and -22% max drawdown (unverified, practitioner script). A summary of the 2023 follow-up says the strategy had a rough period and drawdown in 2022-2023 — [Concretum/secondary via search](https://concretumgroup.substack.com/p/bitcoin-trends-around-the-clock); [TradingView EdgeTools](https://www.tradingview.com/script/IzFZxayj/); [PapersWithBacktest](https://paperswithbacktest.com/blog/bitcoin-never-sleeps-exploiting-seasonality)
- Proposed explanation (secondary, not quoted from the paper): 21-23 UTC is when NYSE, LSE and TSE are all closed — [TradingView EdgeTools](https://www.tradingview.com/script/IzFZxayj/)
- Related Quantpedia work maps BTC returns onto NYSE hours with hourly Gemini data (updated to Oct 2024). On NYSE-open days most BTC return accrues in the overnight (non-NYSE) window, and the intraday (NYSE-hours) window is smaller and more volatile. On weekends and holidays the pattern reverses — [Quantpedia: intraday or overnight anomalies](https://quantpedia.com/are-there-seasonal-intraday-or-overnight-anomalies-in-bitcoin/); [Quantpedia: trading BTC overnight sessions](https://quantpedia.com/how-to-profitably-trade-bitcoins-overnight-sessions/); [Quantpedia strategy page](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/intraday-seasonality-in-bitcoin)
- An academic version of the same split (Xiamen University study, reported by CCN; 5-minute data 2018-2023): BTC overnight (non-US-trading-hours) returns average about +0.093% vs about -0.029% during US trading hours. The authors read this as sentiment-driven daily reversal that is stronger when attention is high. The overnight leg also forecasts the VIX (coefficient about -0.262; Gu, Lin & Liu via secondary summary) — [CCN](https://www.ccn.com/news/crypto/bitcoins-overnight-crypto-unlock-new-insights-equity-volatility/); [aligrithm summary](https://aligrithm.com/bitcoins-overnight-returns-forecast-the-vix/)
- Aalto thesis ("Crypto never sleeps, but humans do"): the BTC overnight premium is strongest in speculative, retail-driven periods and fades in bear markets. After US spot BTC ETFs launched (Jan 2024), the overnight premium rose in the US region — [Aalto](https://aaltodoc.aalto.fi/items/e4962f5a-045a-4131-b329-cc27926f6c4f)
- QuantifiedStrategies (practitioner): Monday is the best day to hold BTC, and 21:00-23:00 UTC are the peak hours (unverified) — [QuantifiedStrategies](https://www.quantifiedstrategies.com/bitcoin-intraday-seasonality-trading-strategy-backtest-results/)

**Cross-exchange intraday activity and return pattern**
- Brauneis, Mestel & Theissen (2025), "The crypto world trades at tea time", Review of Quantitative Finance and Accounting 64(1):275-304 (online June 2024; SSRN 4347853). Data: 1,940 pairs on 38 exchanges. Returns are below average about 01:00-05:00 UTC and peak 15:00-16:00 UTC. Activity, volatility and illiquidity peak 16:00-17:00 UTC (overlap of European and US equity hours). The patterns are "remarkably similar" across exchanges, time zones and pairs — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11156-024-01304-1); [NTU repository](https://irep.ntu.ac.uk/id/eprint/51576/)
- Kurdi & Nadarajah (2026, Computational Economics; Binance 1-minute data): daily BTC highs and lows cluster around 18:00 UTC, and minimum volume falls near 00:31 UTC — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10614-026-11438-0)
- Earlier Bitstamp work: intraday volume and volatility peak on Fridays and are lowest on Saturdays, which the authors link to stock-market hours — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/334727071_Time-of-Day_Periodicities_of_Trading_Volume_and_Volatility_in_Bitcoin_Exchange_Does_the_Stock_Market_Matter)
- Eross et al., "The intraday dynamics of bitcoin" (Reading accepted manuscript) is another foundational intraday study — [CentAUR](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/81745/3/2.R&R.Manuscript.pdf)

**Concretum "Monday Asia Open" effect (intraday trend strength by weekday and hour)**
- Zarattini, Pagani & Barbon (Concretum, 30 Jan 2026), "Seasonality in Bitcoin Intraday Trend Trading". Sample 2018-2025. A long-short ensemble of high-frequency intraday trend-following models earns about 1.6 Sharpe gross of fees, vs just below 0.8 for 20%-vol-targeted long-only BTC. Benchmark returns turn strongly positive around Sunday 19:00 New York time (about 23:00-00:00 UTC) and stay elevated for about 24 hours, in line with the Monday open of Asian cash equity markets. The effect is much stronger after mid-2020. Explanations offered: Asian liquidity and positioning flows at the start of the week, and institutional rebalancing — [Concretum](https://concretumgroup.com/seasonality-in-bitcoin-intraday-trend-trading/); [Concretum Substack](https://concretumgroup.substack.com/p/bitcoin-trends-around-the-clock)

**Quarter-hour / turn-of-candle microstructure seasonality**
- Shanaev, Vasenin & Stepanov (2023), "Turn-of-the-candle effect in bitcoin returns", Heliyon (SSRN 4090044). Minutes 0, 15, 30 and 45 of each hour average +0.58 bp per minute, and other minutes average negative. A 2021 simulation net of Bitfinex fees and spreads reports 214% annualised return and Sharpe 4.96, vs 62.95% and 0.77 for buy-and-hold. The effect appears only from mid-to-late 2020 and is attributed to HFT/algorithmic trading — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10015199/); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4090044)
- The Quarter-Hour Effect paper (Q1) documents the same clustering in Binance perps from 2021 to 2024, which partly replicates the mechanism on a different venue — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09426)

**Funding-settlement times (00/08/16 UTC)**
- The only data-driven study found is "Temporal Dynamics of Market Microstructure in Cryptocurrency Perpetual Futures" (MDPI IJFS 14(5):103). Cross-exchange funding spreads peak about 2 hours after standard settlement (02:00, 10:00, 18:00 UTC). The effects are statistically significant but "economically modest". This study covers spreads, not perp price returns — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7072/14/5/103)

**Day-of-week, weekend, turn-of-month**
- Mueller (2024), "Revisiting seasonality in cryptocurrencies", Finance Research Letters 64, 105429. Seasonality evidence in crypto returns "is not robust". The BTC Monday effect is internally valid but does not persist after 2015. The cross-sectional Monday effect is typically negative, with wide confidence intervals. The one robust pattern is lower weekend trading activity across 500 coins — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v64y2024ics1544612324004598.html); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/379995324_Revisiting_seasonality_in_cryptocurrencies)
- BTC weekend effect, 2014-2024: no detectable weekend-weekday gap in mean returns. Volatility and volume are lower on weekends — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/396418897_Bitcoin's_Weekend_Effect_Returns_Volatility_and_Volume_2014-2024)
- "Retail weekends, institutional weekdays: liquidity and return asymmetries in cryptocurrency markets" (2026, ScienceDirect) documents weekend/weekday liquidity asymmetry — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1062976926001407)
- Turn-of-month: the evidence is inconsistent. One study finds a BTC-only effect, another finds none — [ResearchGate TOM](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/359327404_Turn-of-the-month_effect_in_cryptocurrencies); [FRL 2021 calendar effects](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1544612321003597)
- Earlier day-of-week studies (Caporale & Plastun 2019 type; FRL 2018 "Bitcoin and the day-of-the-week effect") predate the sample window and are superseded by Mueller 2024 — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1544612317307894); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/329050986_The_day_of_the_week_effect_in_the_cryptocurrency_market)
- Cross-sectional weekday seasonality: Long, Zaremba, Demir, Szczygielski & Vasenin (2020, FRL 35:101566), 151 coins 2016-2019. A coin's past returns on the same weekday predict its relative performance on that weekday, and this is not explained by momentum, size, beta, IVOL or liquidity — [ScienceDirect (open access)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S154461232030235X)

### Inferences
- No new peer-reviewed paper found replicates 21-23 UTC on post-2022 data or on Binance perps. The originating sample (Gemini to Feb 2022) overlaps the project's 2020-2022 data, so only 2022-2026 is truly out of sample for the project. The reported 2022-2023 drawdown is a warning sign. The more general "BTC return accrues outside NYSE cash hours" finding (Quantpedia to Oct 2024; Xiamen 2018-2023; Aalto post-ETF) is the economically grounded version: 21-23 UTC sits inside the post-US-close overnight window. A robustness test the project can run: does the 20:00 (US close) to 13:30 UTC (US open) window beat the US-hours window on the 20 Binance perps 2022-2026? If 21-23 is merely the best-looking slice of that window, prefer the broader, less-mined window.
- 21-23 UTC is also around the funding-adjacent hours only loosely (next funding at 00:00 UTC), and the Concretum Monday-Asia-open effect starts about 23:00-00:00 UTC on Sunday. Pre-register a check that 21-23 UTC returns are not concentrated on Sundays (which would make it a Monday-Asia-open artefact).
- Sharpe 4.96 for turn-of-candle on one exchange's 2021 simulation looks implausible net of perp costs and should be treated as data-mined until replicated. Minute-level holding needs dozens of round trips per day.
- Day-of-week, weekend and turn-of-month return effects should be deprioritised (Mueller 2024 negative result).

### Gaps
- No primary-source data on BTC/ETH perp returns before and after 00/08/16 UTC funding settlements. The only study covers spreads. This is easily tested in-house with 1-minute data.
- The exact post-2022 performance of 21-23 UTC was not found in a primary source, nor were Vojtko's own causal explanation or the Beluská & Vojtko 2024 update results.
- Concretum's net-of-cost numbers and exact model specifications for the Monday Asia Open effect were not visible.

## Q3. Which cross-sectional crypto factors held up out of sample after publication (2022-2026)? Including Liu, Tsyvinski & Wu (2022 JF) and replications.

### Takeaway
LTW's three-factor model (market, size, momentum) is the reference. Size is unattractive on a 20-large-coin universe, and momentum shows crash risk and a period of failure (2022-2023). CTREND (Fieberg et al., JFQA 2025) is the best-documented factor with built-in OOS design and claimed survival in big, liquid coins after costs, but its evaluation ends May 2022. Short-term (daily) reversal is a liquidity-provision premium concentrated in illiquid coins, and the largest coins show daily momentum instead. Lottery/MAX is priced but weak in magnitude. None found has clean, independent post-2022 OOS evidence on large-cap perps.

### Cited Findings
**Liu, Tsyvinski & Wu (2022), "Common Risk Factors in Cryptocurrency", Journal of Finance 77(2) (NBER w25882)**
- A three-factor model (crypto market, size, momentum) explains the cross-section. Ten characteristics form significant long-short strategies (size, momentum, volume and volatility groups), all of which are spanned by the three factors — [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25882); [NBER PDF](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w25882/w25882.pdf); [Yale](https://economics.yale.edu/research/common-risk-factors-cryptocurrency)
- Secondary citation: weekly long-short momentum in LTW earns about 3% per week (unverified against the original) — [ResearchGate (Up or down)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/354881251_Up_or_down_Short-term_reversal_momentum_and_liquidity_effects_in_cryptocurrency_markets)
- Post-publication evidence:
  - A 2025 arXiv paper ("Cryptocurrency as an Investable Asset Class: Coming of Age") reports size, value and momentum long-short results consistent with LTW and robust in the post-2020 sample — [arXiv 2510.14435](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.14435)
  - Conflicting: a Springer chapter reports that all momentum strategies failed in 2022-2023, and that momentum factors also failed to price returns in 2014-2018 — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-95-2566-9_30)
  - LTW's three factors cannot explain time-series momentum, which is attributed to an overreaction factor — [AUT working paper](https://acfr.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0009/918729/Time_Series_and_Cross_Sectional_Momentum_in_the_Cryptocurrency_Market_with_IA.pdf)
  - "Cryptocurrency Momentum: Is It an Illusion?" (Int. J. Finance & Economics, 2026) questions momentum robustness — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/ijfiec/v31y2026i2p2180-2193.html)
- Momentum crash risk: Grobys, Kolari, Sandretto, Shahzad & Äijö (2025), "Cryptocurrency momentum has (not) its moments", Financial Markets and Portfolio Management 39. Large-cap crypto momentum suffers severe crashes, including about -255% in Dec 2020 from one coin's jump in the short leg. Volatility scaling improves payoffs, but power-law tests suggest the tail variance is undefined, so risk management does not remove tail risk — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11408-025-00474-9); [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/kap/fmktpm/v39y2025i4d10.1007_s11408-025-00474-9.html)
- Factor momentum: winner factors keep outperforming 2014-2022, earning about 0.52% per week, though cited work disputes its superiority — [Cryptocurrency Factor Momentum](https://open.icm.edu.pl/server/api/core/bitstreams/86a51c47-8cd3-4201-88ee-42f44fb89227/content)
- Non-standard errors: crypto cross-sectional results vary greatly with researcher design choices, described as a new "factor zoo" — [IRFA 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1057521924000383)

**CTREND: Fieberg, Liedtke, Poddig, Walker & Zaremba, "A Trend Factor for the Cross-Section of Cryptocurrency Returns", JFQA (2025; SSRN 4601972, 2023)**
- Combines price and volume technical signals across horizons with machine learning on more than 3,000 coins, using out-of-sample estimates April 2016 to May 2022. Not subsumed by known factors. Robust across subperiods and market states, survives transaction costs, and persists in big, liquid coins — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4601972); [JFQA PDF](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/4C1509ACBA33D5DCAF0AC24379148178/S0022109024000747a.pdf/trend_factor_for_the_cross_section_of_cryptocurrency_returns.pdf); [Corvinus repository](https://unipub.lib.uni-corvinus.hu/11621/1/a-trend-factor-for-the-cross-section-of-cryptocurrency-returns.pdf)
- Reported performance: about 1.89-2.44% per week for a CTREND factor built on the largest 100 coins; another version cites 3.87% per week for the full universe. A $1 price filter cuts the median Sharpe from 1.64 to 1.13. The versions are not directly comparable — [search excerpts of SSRN/JFQA](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4601972)
- Weekly rebalancing. Inputs are price and volume moving-average signals, which the project's data can build.

**Short-term reversal / daily momentum and liquidity**
- Zaremba, Bilgin, Long, Mercik & Szczygielski (2021), "Up or down? Short-term reversal, momentum, and liquidity effects in cryptocurrency markets", IRFA 78. Data: more than 3,600 coins, daily. Coins with low prior-day return outperform those with high prior-day return. The reversal stems from illiquidity, and the largest, most liquid coins show daily momentum instead — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1057521921002349); [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finana/v78y2021ics1057521921002349.html)
- Bianchi, Babiak & Dickerson (2022), "Trading volume and liquidity provision in cryptocurrency markets", J. Banking & Finance 142:106547. USD pairs on several CEXs, Mar 2017 to Mar 2022. Short-term reversal returns, read as liquidity-provision profits, are concentrated in pairs with lower activity, in line with inventory-risk and adverse-selection theories — [CERGE-EI WP](https://www.cerge-ei.cz/pdf/wp/Wp730.pdf); [Warwick](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/165914/)
- Follow-up, "Returns from liquidity provision in cryptocurrency markets" (JBF 175, 2025): a higher liquidity-provision premium coincides with lower liquidity, volume and trade counts, plus more Uniswap withdrawals, higher fees and greater impermanent loss — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbfina/v175y2025ics0378426625000317.html)
- Newer reversal variants: Nakagawa & Sakemoto (2025, FRL 85), "New behaviorally-based cross-sectional reversal portfolios ... and market uncertainty" — [SSRN](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.5001299); "Cross-sectional interactions in cryptocurrency returns" (IRFA 97, 2025) — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finana/v97y2025ics1057521924007415.html)

**Lottery / MAX / IVOL / skewness**
- MAX effect: a significant MAX effect and an insignificant volatility effect appear in cross-sectional regressions, with the caveat that MAX may substitute for volatility (Financial Innovation 2021) — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40854-021-00291-9)
- Lottery-like demand is priced in weekly crypto cross-sections, while the IVOL effect is economically small (J. Int. Financial Markets, Inst. & Money 2021) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1042443121000081)
- Intraday MAX on the top 100 liquid coins: a one-standard-deviation rise in MAX predicts a 0.043% lower subsequent return (Studies in Economics and Finance, 2025) — [Emerald](https://www.emerald.com/sef/article-abstract/42/4/799/1256079/Intraday-lottery-demands-in-cryptocurrency-market?redirectedFrom=PDF)
- Once MAX is included, skewness predictability weakens a lot (FRL 2020) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1544612320303135)

**Abnormal volume (disagreement)**: negatively priced in the cross-section (Garfinkel et al., Financial Management 2025) — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/fima.12491)

### Inferences
- On a 20-coin large-cap perp universe: size is nearly degenerate. Daily reversal is predicted *not* to work, since it lives in illiquid coins, and Zaremba et al. predict daily *momentum* for the largest coins. The project's earlier rejection of 1-30 day XS momentum/reversal on 58 OKX perps is consistent with the momentum-crash and 2022-2023 failure evidence. CTREND is the strongest candidate to re-test, because it (a) claims survival in big coins after costs, (b) is a combination signal rather than a single lookback, and (c) has an OOS design. Its post-May-2022 performance is unknown. Treat 2022-2026 on Binance perps as a genuine holdout.
- Abnormal-volume (negative), MAX (negative) and same-weekday seasonality are cheap secondary sorts from the project's data. With 20 coins, use quintile or tercile long-short or rank-weighted portfolios, and expect low t-stats.
- Data-mining flag: crypto cross-sectional results show large non-standard errors (IRFA 2024), so pre-register universe, rebalance day and weighting.

### Gaps

- No post-publication (after May 2022) CTREND performance evidence was found.
- Network factors (for example, the LTW / Liu-Tsyvinski "Risks and Returns" network/adoption factors) were not covered by searches. The project's data lacks on-chain inputs anyway.
- I could not see the exact CTREND signal list or ML (elastic net / combination) specification.

## Q4. Intraday breakout / volatility-expansion / opening-range style strategies on BTC with net results

### Takeaway
The best-documented intraday BTC trend work is Concretum's (2025-2026): an ensemble of intraday trend models with about 1.6 gross Sharpe for 2018-2025, concentrated around the Sunday-night/Monday Asia open. Net-of-cost figures were not visible. Shen, Urquhart & Wang (2022) document first-half-hour to last-half-hour BTC intraday momentum on volume-defined sessions (close to the rejected Gao et al. half-hour momentum). The Zarattini "Beat the Market" noise-area breakout is net-of-cost documented only for SPY.

### Cited Findings
- Zarattini, Aziz & Barbon (2024), "Beat the Market: An Effective Intraday Momentum Strategy for S&P500 ETF (SPY)" (SSRN 4824172). A noise-area (Concretum Bands) breakout with dynamic trailing stops earns 19.6% annualised with Sharpe 1.33 net of costs, 2007 to early 2024. This is SPY, not BTC — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4824172); [Quant macro review](https://quantmacro.substack.com/p/paper-review-an-effective-intraday)
- Concretum (Zarattini, Pagani, Barbon; Jan 2026) applies intraday trend-following (Concretum Bands ensemble) to BTC 2018-2025. Gross Sharpe is about 1.6 vs about 0.8 for vol-targeted long-only BTC. Strength concentrates from Sunday 19:00 NY for about 24 hours (Monday Asia open) and is more pronounced after mid-2020 — [Concretum](https://concretumgroup.com/seasonality-in-bitcoin-intraday-trend-trading/); [Concretum X post](https://x.com/ConcretumR/status/1839357756824023338?lang=en)
- Shen, Urquhart & Wang (2022), "Bitcoin intraday time series momentum", Financial Review 57(2):319-344. With volume as a proxy for market time, the first half-hour return predicts the last half-hour return. The effect is strongest in high-volume and high-volatility sessions, profitable especially in downturns, and driven by liquidity provision rather than late-informed trading. A preprint mentions about 7.8% annualised (unverified) — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/fire.12290); [CentAUR accepted version](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/100181/3/21Sep2021Bitcoin%20Intraday%20Time-Series%20Momentum.R2.pdf)
- Lagged momentum and reversal strategies across daytime vs overnight sessions in BTC and ETH (JRFM 19(9):692) — [MDPI DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/jrfm19090692)
- Padysak & Vojtko (2022) / Quantpedia: BTC trends near local maxima (buy on a new N-day high) and mean-reverts near minima. The combined strategy is reported with return-to-volatility above 2 (Quantpedia, unverified) — [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/trend-following-and-mean-reversion-in-bitcoin/); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4081000)

### Inferences
- The Concretum intraday-trend ensemble is the most promising intraday breakout family for BTC perps. It is long-short and intraday (flat overnight-style risk), and its gross Sharpe is double that of long-only BTC. A direct test: port the SPY noise-area rule to BTC by defining the "session open" as 00:00 UTC (or the Sunday 23:00 UTC Asia open). Use 1-minute bars, a sigma band from the 14-day average absolute move from the open at the same minute, entry on band break, trailing stop at max(band, VWAP), and flat at session end. Charge about 4-5 bp per side plus slippage.
- Shen-Urquhart-Wang is mechanically close to the rejected Gao et al. half-hour momentum. Skip it unless the volume-clock session definition is materially different.

### Gaps
- No found source gives BTC opening-range-breakout or volatility-expansion results net of perp fees, nor Concretum BTC net figures.
- No independent replication of the Concretum BTC result was found.

## Q5. Which of these are likely low-correlated with a long-only daily crypto trend strategy?

### Takeaway
The lowest expected correlation comes from market-neutral cross-sectional (CTREND, abnormal volume, MAX) and long-short intraday-trend sleeves (Concretum), plus very short-hold time-of-day windows. A long-only time-of-day window (21-23 UTC) holds BTC only 2 of 24 hours, so its correlation to daily trend is low in level terms. It is still long-beta and conditionally stronger in bull and speculative regimes, so it will likely lose together with trend in bear markets.

### Cited Findings
- Concretum's intraday trend ensemble is long-short. Its Sharpe (about 1.6 gross) is roughly double that of long-only vol-targeted BTC (just below 0.8) over 2018-2025, which suggests a different return source from buy-and-hold beta — [Concretum](https://concretumgroup.com/seasonality-in-bitcoin-intraday-trend-trading/)
- Shen, Urquhart & Wang's BTC intraday momentum is especially profitable during market downturns, which is a hedge-like profile against long-only trend — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/fire.12290)
- CTREND is robust across market states and not subsumed by the market factor — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4601972)
- The BTC overnight premium is strongest in speculative periods and fades in bear markets, so it co-moves with the regimes in which long-only trend also earns — [Aalto](https://aaltodoc.aalto.fi/items/e4962f5a-045a-4131-b329-cc27926f6c4f)
- The 21-23 UTC strategy reportedly suffered drawdowns in 2022-2023, the same bear period in which long-only trend sits mostly flat or loses — [PapersWithBacktest](https://paperswithbacktest.com/blog/bitcoin-never-sleeps-exploiting-seasonality)
- Cross-sectional momentum has extreme idiosyncratic crash risk from single-coin jumps in the short leg, which is unrelated to market direction — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11408-025-00474-9)

### Inferences
- Ranked by expected diversification against long-only daily trend, with plausibility given the evidence:
  1. Long-short intraday BTC/ETH trend (Concretum-style), with optional Sunday-Asia-open emphasis. This is both low-correlated and documented post-2020.
  2. CTREND-style weekly market-neutral cross-section on 20 perps. It has a plausible economic basis and claimed big-coin survival, but no post-2022 evidence.
  3. 4-12 hour quarter-hour opening-imbalance signal (long-short). It would be uncorrelated if real, but evidence is weak.
  4. Time-of-day long windows (21-23 UTC, or the US-close-to-US-open window). Their exposure to daily trend is low, but their regime dependence is shared. Consider running them as long-short (long 21-23, short 01-05 UTC, the lowest-return hours per Brauneis et al.) to remove beta.
  5. Reversal after taker bursts. It is uncorrelated, but costs kill it.
- These correlation statements are inferred from strategy structure and the regime findings above. None of the sources reports a correlation with a daily crypto trend strategy, so the project must measure it in-house.

### Gaps
- No source reports correlations between any of these strategies and a long-only daily crypto trend-following strategy.
